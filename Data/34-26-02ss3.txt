t=ALIGNMENT MODE. The Micro IRU automatically selects the correct alignment mode. The two possible modes are: STATIONARY ALIGN. The Stationary Alignment mode is the primary method of alignment. This mode is selected when the aircraft is on the ground and not moving. The time required to complete a stationary alignment varies with latitude, ranging from 5 to 17 minutes. The alignment may fail if the Micro IRU senses excessive motion during the initialization sequence, or if the Micro IRU fails to receive an initialization position. A position may be obtained from the FMS, GPS or entered by the flight crew. ALIGN IN MOTION. The AIM mode initializes the IRS while in flight to recover full IRU navigation capability. This mode will be selected in the event of a power loss while in flight. The time required to complete an AIM varies as a function of airplane motion, and ranges from 15 to 30 minutes. When the Micro IRU is performing an AIM, the flight crew will be notified by an appropriate EICAS message. Stationary Alignment and AIM Modes are performed in conjunction with the attitude mode prior to entry into the navigation mode. In order to perform an AIM, the IRU requires GPS valid data. Should the AIM fail, the Micro IRU will remain in attitude mode, with attitude data available but with positional data unavailable.
